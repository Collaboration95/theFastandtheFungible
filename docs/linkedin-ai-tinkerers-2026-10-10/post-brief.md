# Post brief

## Audience

Technical builders, researchers, engineers, and AI product people who may attend AI Tinkerers or follow the event afterward. They are more likely to engage with a concrete design problem and a reusable pattern than with a generic “excited to announce” update.

## Recommended positioning

Lead with **evidence economics** and use **agent accountability** as the stakes:

> A research agent is not trustworthy because it can spend money. It is trustworthy when it can show what it refused to buy, why it bought what it did, and how that evidence changed the conclusion.

This fits the event’s “show the build / teach, don’t sell” standard and gives readers a reason to continue reading.

## Reader value to give away

Offer a portable rule builders can reuse for any spending agent:

> Buy only when the next source is non-redundant, inside the mandate, and likely to change or materially strengthen a named claim enough to justify its price. Otherwise, skip or block it.

Then make the rule concrete with the workflow:

`mandate → open baseline → evidence families → named gap → marginal-value comparison → approval → unlock → impact diff → receipt`

## Suggested post arc

1. Hook with the refusal: the most interesting demo moment may be the duplicate that is skipped or the over-ceiling source that is blocked.
2. Announce the October 10 AI Tinkerers Singapore demo and the project name.
3. Explain the live flow in one compact paragraph.
4. Share the reusable decision rule and why evidence-family clustering matters.
5. Mention how Codex and editable Excalidraw diagrams are being used to make the workflow and boundaries legible.
6. Invite specific critique: Is marginal evidence value a defensible ranking signal? Where would evidence-family clustering fail? Is a self-issued receipt enough accountability?
7. Link the event and the project homepage when available.

## Visual plan

Use one polished homepage screenshot as the attention image, then make the post’s value come from a simple diagram or carousel panel:

- Panel 1: the workflow from mandate to receipt.
- Panel 2: the three judgment calls — buy, skip, block.
- Panel 3: before/after conclusion and the evidence span that changed it.

The event guidance says a single architecture diagram is fine when it clarifies the build. Avoid a collage of UI screenshots or a marketing hero that hides the internals.

## CTA

Ask for critique, not applause. A good ending is: “If you build retrieval, agents, or evaluation systems, I’d especially value your view on where this decision rule breaks.” Then include the event link and the homepage link.
