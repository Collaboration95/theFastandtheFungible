Overview · audit and direction

Let the room watch it decide.

ResearchAgent’s best moment lasts 40 milliseconds and happens behind a tab. A decision model prices evidence, code pays for it, and the answer changes. Today the audience lands on the finished answer and never sees the decision. This proposal puts the whole run on one screen, paces it for people, and gives the product a look and a motion language of its own. Light theme only.

What the 5 Oct build did

I ran npm run demo (fixture providers) on 5 Oct and captured every state at 1440 × 900. The five gates hold up well: labels are present, citations resolve, retries charge once. The problems are about flow and legibility. Click a screenshot to see it full size.

Six moves

  1. Pace the run for people. Replay the persisted trace through a queue with a minimum time per step. A 40 ms fixture run then reads as a 30-second story; real pace is one toggle away in the presenter menu. P1
  2. One screen, three zones. The run tape on the left says what it’s doing. The answer in the middle says what it knows. The wallet on the right says what it’s spending and why. No tabs. B3
  3. Answer first. A one-sentence short answer with citations, then the open gap, then claims grouped by stance. C3
  4. Plain words on top, raw data one key away. Verdict stamps with plain reasons on the surface. Press W for the trace, wire, policy and receipts. H5
  5. Say when it’s done. The tape, a toast, the tab title, the favicon, and a desktop notification if the tab is hidden. Notify
  6. A brand that moves. A blue pen for the agent’s marks, a highlighter for evidence, a coin between citation brackets. Motion only where something real happens. Brand

What I looked at

  • Perplexity Advanced Deep Research: a progress display that shows which sources are read and lets findings appear before the report is done. Source for the run tape and strip.
  • Deep research UIs compared: Manus shows every step but buries the result; Gemini keeps the result central. The three-zone layout takes Gemini’s focus with Manus’s visibility.
  • Smashing: interface patterns for AI transparency: dynamic checklist (the tape), thinking toggle (Show work), partial success (failed delivery), audit trail (receipts).
  • Family: “Family Values”: fluid interfaces where elements turn into each other. Source for the composer lifting into the heading and the Ask button turning into a status.
  • Emil Kowalski: building a toast component: stacked toasts that tuck behind each other and use interruptible transitions.
  • x402 whitepaper: the 402 → payment → resource shape the wire track draws.

Review · v1.3 · stage rehearsal, 9 Oct

Point at five things. Leave the rest.

Three reviewers played an audience member, the presenter and a skeptical engineer, and ran the live demo on main thirteen times: a random question to prove it is live, then the demo questions. This tab is what they found: how cluttered it is, what to point at and what to skip, a ten-minute script, a ranked list of simplifications, and alternatives for each of them to vote on.

About the reviewers. They were AI agents (Sonnet 5.5) driving the browser, not people in a room. Their timings are good to a second or two, and their reading of “clutter” is a judgement, not a measurement. The Stop finding was checked against the server’s own event log. Run each fix past a person before it ships.

Is it too cluttered?

Yes at the end, no at the start. Home is clean and the motion works. The last screen carries about 60 separate things, and the ones that carry the story are the smallest.

What the numbers say

  • The shipped Done screen has ; the v1.1 mockup of the same moment has , and the first v1 proposal . The build is no lighter than the proposal it followed.
  • Right rail: 12 decision rows, most of them the same writer’s name, at 9–11 px.
  • The row that matters (the BUY) is one of twelve and sits in the same type as the others.

What reads from the back of a room

  • The home heading, the big budget number, the yellow highlight on v2, “Done · S$0.90 of S$2.00”.
  • Everything in the right rail below the budget does not.
  • At 1280 × 720 the refund card, the round-2 rows and the Writers trust columns fall off the screen.

What to point at, and what not to

Point at

  • Home. “Ask a question. Give it a budget.” and the budget chip.
  • The big budget number counting S$2.00 → S$1.10. Money visibly moves.
  • The yellow v2 answer with “Qualifies the free answer” and Compare v1 → v2.
  • The receipt (View): the best proof object. Invoice, ledger link, “Charges 1”, the striped “XRPL TESTNET · no real value”.
  • Show work → Policy if asked how it chose: probabilities, “only code purchases”.
  • UC3 only: the red “Proof failed”, the green “Refunded S$0.30” and the BLOCKED row on the re-ask.

Do not point at

  • The short answer, when the question is off-topic. It answers with whatever is nearest in the corpus. Point at the open gap and “S$0.00 spent” instead.
  • The 12-row decision list. Repeated names, no titles. It looks like a bug.
  • The v2 headline and the “Gap closed” sentence (they repeat v1, and splice two sentences).
  • The Writers tab. Clipped columns, “T 0.03”, a trust score you will have to say out loud.
  • The bottom stepper, “T 0.80” chips, Wire, the yellow “i”. Unlabeled, tiny, or does nothing visible.
  • “OpenAI Decisions · gpt-6-luna” while you say Clef. Say “decision model”.

What could go wrong on stage

1Stop does not stop a purchase already in flight.

Checked against the event log on three UC3 runs. No purchase started after Stop was accepted, so the gate holds. But Stop at about 12 s still ended with S$0.40 paid, an article never read and no v2, under the words “stopped by you”. The clean window is roughly the first 6 s after the plan card closes. A presenter pressing Stop for effect will spend money.

2The screen can say the opposite of the server.

At Stage 1× pace the UI kept “Nothing paywalled was worth buying” and “S$0.00 spent” for about 10 s after the server had bought. Say “watch” and talk over it, or switch pace to Real.

3UC4 spent S$0.60 in one review run.

The handoff says UC4 finds its missing fact for free and spends S$0. In the skeptic’s run the free follow-up left 2 of 3 facts, a purchase made it 3 of 3, and the screen still said “found free on a focused search”. The same question went S$0 in the final smoke, so it varies.

4Cloudflare’s free quota.

The primary account’s 10,000 daily neurons ran out. Live search fell back to keyword only and the header showed “Fixture fallback · Search”. That is the fallback label working, but it undermines “live”. Run with CF_BACKUP=1 until 08:00 SGT.

5A random question gets a confident, irrelevant answer.

“What time is it, and what are you?” returned a short answer about rack power draw and six “challenges” to irrelevant records. Honest in the gap (“no evidence states what the system is”), strange in the headline.

6Dead air.

0–5 s after Ask (“Starting…”), up to 26 s if the clarify card waits, and about 6 s between the refund and round 2 in UC3. UC3 took 69 s at Stage 1× against 34 s in the live smoke.

✓What held up.

No console errors. The receipt, Show work → Policy and the citation drawer all survived poking. “Proof verified · 6 claims recomputed” matches the server’s proof event. The five gates were not broken in any run.

Tweaks · v1.1 · feedback round 1

Less on screen, same story

First feedback on v1: a new user sees too much at once, the top bar is full of engine names, and the layout should be the one people already know, with a sidebar on the left, a bar along the bottom and a panel on the right. Below are five small moves on top of v1, not a redesign. The motion stays; panels tidy up after their moment instead of skipping it.

What we heard

1Too much information for a new user.

F2 alone shows , eleven timed steps, four value bars on an axis, a five-node wire track and a 64-character hash. The story is in there, along with everything else.

2No need for Research, Decide, Publisher or Simulated SGD up top.

Model names and the publisher host are engine facts. They matter to the team and to the talk, not to someone asking a question.

3Use the layout every SaaS and LLM product uses.

A sidebar on the left that expands, a bar along the bottom for the work, a panel on the right. People already know where to look.

Where I agree, and one limit

  • Agree: the chips go. A model name only matters when that model fails, so a chip appears only then, amber, with the reason. The full list moves to Show work → Models.
  • One limit: “simulated” can’t go. Labelling simulated money is hard gate 5. T1 moves the label onto the money instead: the budget card, the coins on Home, receipts and money toasts. The label sits on the thing that’s simulated. AB3 checks that nobody misses it.
  • Agree: the familiar shell. New question and past runs on the left, the run along the bottom, budget and purchases on the right. Sign-in slots into the sidebar’s foot later.
  • Watch: bars along the bottom are easy to miss. Fine for progress, risky for the purchase, which is the point of the demo. The purchase still announces itself in the right panel and with a toast, and AB1 tests whether people notice it.
  • The motion stays. Bars grow and stamps land exactly as in v1. Once the purchase is verified, the panel folds to results. Show the moment, then tidy up.

A/B flows

Four small tests for the open questions. Both variants start from the recommended tweaks and differ in one thing. Each is a three-screen flow: click a screen to open it large, then “Play into this screen” to see its motion.

Running the tests

  1. Five people who haven’t seen ResearchAgent. Teammates who know v1 can’t unsee it, so they give feedback rather than test.
  2. Ten minutes each, on a laptop at 100% zoom, in person or over screen share.
  3. Alternate the order. Odd-numbered testers see A first, even-numbered testers see B first.
  4. Play each flow rather than showing stills. The motion is part of what’s tested.
  5. Read the task, then stay quiet. Note the answer, the seconds, and where they looked first.
  6. Decide with the rule written on the test, not by counting votes.

Flow · build spec

Every screen, and how you get there

38 screens in 11 groups. The mockups follow the “Mockups show” switch (the shipped v1.2 by default); the written behaviour is still the v1 proposal, so where they differ, the Shipped tab is right. Each has a code, what leads to it, what you can do there and where that goes. Click a screen to open it large. In the large view, “Play into this screen” animates the step that leads to it.

Route map

The thick line is the 10 Oct demo story. Branches drop off it; the bottom row can happen at any step. Click a station to jump to its screen.

Moving between screens

TakeoffHome → workspace

The composer lifts and shrinks into the question heading, 420 ms, cubic-bezier(.6,0,.3,1). Columns slide in 60 ms apart.

Step revealwithin a run

New things rise 10 px and fade in, 450 ms ease-out. The tape node pops. Nothing already on screen moves.

Stampverdicts, impact

Scale 2.1 → 1 with a slight overshoot and −3° tilt, 450 ms. Stamps in a set land 480 ms apart.

Drawerpassages, previews

Slides in from the right 26 px with a fade, 420 ms. The page behind dims to 20% and blurs 2 px. Esc closes it.

SheetShow work

Rises from the bottom, 420 ms. It covers the lower half, so the short answer stays visible above it.

Toastresults

Rises 40 px and scales 0.94 → 1, 500 ms. Older toasts tuck 14 px behind and shrink 5% each, at most 3.

Moneywallet, receipts

Amounts count down over 900 ms on an ease-out curve. Digests scramble, then lock left to right.

Reduce Motioneverything

All of the above become cuts. Counters jump to their final value; pulsing and flipping stop.

    A2

    Motion · when and how things move

    Motion only where something real happens

    Most SaaS motion is decoration: things fade in because they can. Here every animation stands for an event in the run: a source arrives, a model scores, a verdict lands, money moves, a digest checks out. If nothing happened, nothing moves.

    Four rules

    1. Show the step, then the result. The tape lights before the answer changes, so cause comes before effect.
    2. The pen is the agent; the highlighter is evidence. Blue strokes mark what the agent did. Yellow sweeps mark what a source says.
    3. Calm by default, one big moment. The purchase (402, hold, settle, digest, stamp) gets the orchestrated sequence. Everything else is a 450 ms rise.
    4. Money never jumps. Amounts count, holds hatch then fill, receipts print. People should be able to follow every cent.

    Toasts

    Fire a few. New ones land in front; older ones tuck behind. Hover the stack to fan it out.

    The browser tab

    Desktop notification

    Ask in context, never on load. The checkbox under the composer (“Tell me when it’s done”) requests permission the first time it’s ticked. If the browser blocks it, the checkbox greys out and says so.

    One per run. Only the final result, only when the tab is hidden. The body is the short answer and the spend, so it’s useful without opening the tab.

    Clicking it focuses the tab on the finished run and clears the favicon dot.

    Rules

    • Toast results, not progress. Progress belongs on the tape.
    • Anything about money shows the amount and the SIMULATED label.
    • Errors stay until they’re resolved. Successes leave after 6 s unless they carry the next action.
    • At most 3 toasts are visible. Older ones tuck behind; hover fans them out.
    • The title and favicon dot reset when the tab gets focus again.
    • Sound is off by default. If turned on, one soft tick when the answer is ready, nothing else.
