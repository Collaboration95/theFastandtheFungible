SINGHACKS 2026 — RIPPLE TRACK PROTOTYPE IDEAS
Updated: 5 September 2026

These are the three active alternatives to the parked DueProof concept.
Each must pass two tests:

1. Remove the agent: does the experience collapse into an ordinary app?
2. Replace XRPL/x402 with a card or API key: does the autonomous workflow or
   business model materially weaken?


==========================================================================
IDEA 1 — FAIRCUT
==========================================================================

Pitch
-----
An AI editor licenses the exact seconds it uses—and the creator gets paid
immediately.

Person
------
Leah is a Singapore-based independent filmmaker finishing a 20-second travel
campaign at midnight. She needs a 12-second music cue that can be used
commercially in Singapore and Japan. She cannot spend an hour interpreting
catalogue licences, and she does not want her editor to use an unlicensed or
provenance-unknown track.

Problem
-------
AI can assemble media faster than people can clear the underlying rights.
Existing licensing is usually organized around large catalogues,
subscriptions, and human checkout flows. Independent creators also face
transaction costs that make tiny, narrowly scoped licences uneconomic.

Agent mandate
-------------
"Find a tense but hopeful 12-second cue for this cut. Commercial social use in
Singapore and Japan for six months. Budget S$1. No voice replicas and no asset
without verifiable provenance."

What the agent does
-------------------
1. Understands the edit's timing, mood, and sonic gaps.
2. Discovers independent licensed stems from multiple providers.
3. Auditions watermarked previews against the actual cut.
4. Parses permissions, prohibitions, attribution duties, territory, term,
   provenance, and price.
5. Rejects creatively strong tracks that do not satisfy the rights mandate.
6. Purchases the best eligible 12-second licence.
7. Inserts the clean stem and returns the finished cut plus a rights receipt.

What is purchased
-----------------
One x402-protected package containing:

- the clean 12-second audio stem;
- an ISCC content identifier or cryptographic asset hash;
- a machine-readable ODRL licence;
- a C2PA Content Credentials provenance manifest, where available;
- creator/payee identity and required attribution text;
- licence and delivery hashes.

Why Ripple, t54, and ClawCredit belong
--------------------------------------
- XRPL enables a genuinely small, cross-border, machine-native purchase and
  immediate creator settlement. The payment binds the licence order; private
  media and full licence details remain off-ledger.
- t54/Trustline checks the mandate, asset identifier, rights-holder payee,
  price, territory, term, and delivered licence. Changed rights invalidate the
  original approval.
- t54's Agentic Risk Standard supplies the fee lifecycle: authorize payment,
  deliver the asset and licence, evaluate their hashes and rights, then mark
  the job fulfilled or exceptional.
- ClawCredit is an optional path for an eligible editor agent without a
  pre-funded wallet. Do not make it critical unless live onboarding, payment,
  and repayment are proven.

Industry standards
------------------
- W3C ODRL: permissions, prohibitions, constraints, payment and attribution
  duties — https://www.w3.org/TR/odrl-model/
- ISO 24138:2024 ISCC: identification of digital media assets —
  https://committee.iso.org/standard/77899.html
- C2PA Content Credentials: signed media provenance and edit history —
  https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/

Blocked path
------------
The most attractive S$0.20 track permits personal use only and its C2PA signer
does not match the claimed rights-holder:

BLOCKED
Creative match: strong
Commercial rights: absent
Rights-holder/payee binding: failed
No payment was signed or submitted.

The agent selects a S$0.60 eligible cue, pays on XRP Testnet, unlocks it, and
the audience hears the finished cut.

Three-minute demo
-----------------
1. Play the silent or rough 20-second edit.
2. Show three watermarked cues evaluated inside the cut.
3. Block the most attractive cue for a rights mismatch.
4. Show the eligible licence and Leah's bounded mandate.
5. Pay on XRP Testnet through one pinned x402 flow.
6. Replace the watermark with the clean stem and play the final cut.
7. Open the receipt: user intent -> ODRL rights -> XRPL hash -> asset hash ->
   creator paid.

Irreducibility test
-------------------
- Without the agent, FairCut becomes manual stock-music search and checkout.
  The creative decision inside the actual edit disappears.
- A centralized catalogue could use cards. The MVP must therefore show
  independent providers and a truly small per-use purchase with immediate
  creator settlement, not one fake marketplace balance.

Primary risks
-------------
- C2PA proves signed provenance assertions, not copyright ownership.
- Use original media owned by a consenting creator and a deliberately narrow
  demo licence; do not make unsupported legal claims.
- A single self-hosted catalogue weakens the XRPL argument.

Strategic score: 43/45
Best quality: most elegant and strongest software-only stage transformation.


==========================================================================
IDEA 2 — FINISHLINE
==========================================================================

Pitch
-----
Agents get paid for verified outcomes, not promises.

Person
------
Maya runs a two-person SaaS company. Her Japanese launch is tomorrow morning,
but the checkout page is still English. Her own agent can coordinate the work
but is not qualified to localize Japanese commerce copy. She needs to hire a
specialist agent tonight without trusting a marketplace rating or paying for
an unusable result.

Problem
-------
Agent-to-agent service markets can move quickly, but ordinary payment proves
only that money moved. It does not prove that work met the brief. Providers
also need confidence that successful work will be paid. This is the problem
t54's Agentic Risk Standard directly studies.

Agent mandate
-------------
"Deliver a Japanese XLIFF package for checkout by 8 a.m. Preserve variables
and HTML, use the approved terminology list, pass all structural checks, and
spend at most S$8."

What the orchestrator does
--------------------------
1. Converts Maya's goal into objective acceptance tests.
2. Discovers localization agents and compares expertise, price, SLA, prior
   verified outcomes, and requested collateral.
3. Negotiates a bounded job agreement.
4. Selects a provider on risk-adjusted value, not cheapest price.
5. Monitors delivery and invokes an independent evaluator.
6. Releases or refunds the fee according to the verified result.
7. Returns the translated checkout and an outcome receipt.

What is purchased
-----------------
A single, objectively testable localization job:

- source and target in XLIFF 2.1;
- fixed glossary and prohibited translations;
- variable and HTML preservation;
- 100% segment coverage;
- screenshot render with no overflow;
- delivery deadline and artifact hashes.

Research implementation
-----------------------
FinishLine adapts the t54 Agentic Risk Standard fee track to XRPL:

REQUEST
  -> signed agreement
  -> fee locked
  -> deliverable submitted
  -> independent evaluator verdict
  -> release to provider | refund to Maya
  -> outcome receipt

It also applies Ripple's lending-design principle: judgment remains off-chain
while standardized execution happens on-chain. Translation and structural
quality are evaluated off-chain; XRPL executes the already-decided release or
refund.

Why Ripple, t54, and ClawCredit belong
--------------------------------------
- XRPL provides non-custodial conditional fee settlement. XRP Testnet
  EscrowCreate and EscrowFinish show substantially more technical depth than a
  decorative Payment.
- t54 ARS supplies the roles, signed events, fee lifecycle, evaluator, and
  outcome/accountability model.
- Trustline underwrites Maya's agent, the provider, job scope, fee, evidence,
  and behavioral deviations before value is locked.
- A provider agent may optionally use ClawCredit to buy x402 translation or QA
  compute before it gets paid. This is a second-order service-purchase loop,
  not a general P2P loan.
- AP2 checkout and payment mandates can bind Maya's open constraints to the
  provider's final closed agreement.

Industry standards
------------------
- OASIS XLIFF 2.1: localization interchange and objectively inspectable
  delivery structure —
  https://docs.oasis-open.org/xliff/xliff-core/v2.1/xliff-core-v2.1.pdf
- Agent Payments Protocol (AP2): agent authorization, checkout mandates,
  payment mandates, and receipts —
  https://github.com/google-agentic-commerce/AP2/blob/main/docs/ap2/specification.md
- XRPL conditional Escrow: PREIMAGE-SHA-256 release conditions —
  https://xrpl.org/docs/concepts/payment-types/escrow
- t54 Agentic Risk Standard —
  https://docs.t54.ai/docs/research/agentic-risk-standard

Failure path
------------
The cheapest provider returns an XLIFF file with a broken checkout variable:

DELIVERY FAILED
Payment was locked, not released.
Reason: required variable {{total}} was changed.
Outcome: fee refundable; provider does not receive payment.

A second provider passes the deterministic tests, the localized checkout is
rendered, and its escrow is finished.

Three-minute demo
-----------------
1. Show Maya's English checkout and 8 a.m. mandate.
2. Compare three specialist agents; reject the cheapest on verified outcomes.
3. Display the signed job and exact acceptance tests.
4. Create an XRP Testnet escrow.
5. Receive the XLIFF file and run visible deterministic checks.
6. Reveal the localized checkout.
7. Finish the escrow and show the ARS-style job receipt.

Irreducibility test
-------------------
- Without the agent, FinishLine becomes a freelancer marketplace. The MVP must
  demonstrate adaptive brief construction, provider negotiation, and recovery
  from a failed provider.
- A centralized platform can escrow card funds. The MVP must show open
  providers, no platform custody, native conditional settlement, and an
  independently inspectable release.

Primary risks
-------------
- It has the most moving parts of the three ideas.
- An evaluator can verify structure and terminology, not all linguistic
  quality.
- XRPL's preimage condition is not a native quality oracle. The evaluator is a
  separate trusted role that releases the fulfillment secret.
- Do not mix a direct x402 Payment and escrow without coherent accounting.

Strategic score: 42/45
Best quality: strongest implementation of t54 research and deepest sponsor fit.


==========================================================================
IDEA 3 — LASTPERCENT
==========================================================================

Pitch
-----
At 3% battery, your car agent buys one charge anywhere—without making you join
another network.

Person
------
Aisha is a home-care nurse driving to an urgent evening visit in an unfamiliar
part of Singapore. Her rental EV is at 3%. She cannot safely compare charger
apps while driving, has no account with the nearest operator, and needs enough
energy—not the cheapest headline tariff—to arrive on time.

Problem
-------
Charging infrastructure is expanding rapidly, but discovery, availability,
tariffs, booking, authorization, and payment span different operators. The
challenge is not finding the geographically nearest charger; it is choosing a
compatible, available and trustworthy charging session that meets the
driver's route, time, reserve, and price constraints.

Agent mandate
-------------
"Keep at least 20 km of reserve. When I cannot reach my next destination,
purchase the safest compatible charge that keeps me on schedule. Maximum
S$12, approved licensed operators only, never accept an occupancy fee above
S$0.20 per minute."

What the vehicle agent does
---------------------------
The agent combines:

- location and route;
- battery state, expected consumption, and connector compatibility;
- charger availability and predicted queue;
- per-kWh, per-minute, booking, and roaming fees;
- detour time and Aisha's arrival constraint;
- operator/payee risk and final payment terms.

It chooses and buys a specific reservation and energy package, then delivers
a start authorization to the charger.

What is purchased
-----------------
Use a fixed MVP package rather than open-ended metering:

10 kWh charging authorization
charger: SG-DEMO-014
connector: CCS2
reservation window: 18:40-19:00
maximum session duration: 25 minutes
fixed Testnet price and exact payee

The visible outcome is not just a receipt. A simulated OCPP charger changes
from Reserved to Charging, its energy meter advances, and Aisha's projected
arrival changes from impossible to on time.

Industry-standard bridge
------------------------
OCPI discovery, tariff and reservation
  -> Trustline decision
  -> x402/XRPL payment
  -> OCPP charging authorization and metering
  -> combined payment and energy-delivery receipt

- OCPI: locations, live status, tariffs, authorization, reservation, sessions,
  charging records and roaming — https://evroaming.org/ocpi/
- OCPP: charger-to-management-system communication, transaction handling,
  smart charging, security and reservation —
  https://openchargealliance.org/protocols/open-charge-point-protocol/
- ISO 15118: EV-to-charger charging and control communication —
  https://www.iso.org/standard/77845.html

Why Ripple, t54, and ClawCredit belong
--------------------------------------
- XRPL provides fast direct settlement between a vehicle agent and an
  unfamiliar operator without requiring a bilateral roaming account. Its
  low-energy profile also aligns with the mobility story.
- Trustline checks Aisha's mandate, operator identity, price anomaly, location,
  payee, energy package, timing, and the vehicle agent's permitted role.
- ARS supplies the crucial separation between payment settlement and energy
  delivery. An XRPL hash does not prove that 10 kWh was supplied; the OCPP
  meter receipt closes fulfilment.
- ClawCredit has its most natural role here: an eligible vehicle agent without
  a pre-funded wallet can request credit for the x402 charging service. Limits
  can reflect prior successful sessions and repayments. Current onboarding and
  manual repayment limitations must be stated honestly.
- The idea appeals to Ripple's values of interoperability, low-energy
  settlement, and improving existing infrastructure rather than replacing it.

Blocked path
------------
The closest charger advertises a low tariff, but its final x402 challenge
changes the payee and adds an uncapped occupancy fee:

BLOCKED
Price terms exceed the standing mandate.
Payment destination does not match the licensed operator.
No charging authorization or payment was issued.

The agent chooses the second charger, pays for the fixed package, and starts
the simulated OCPP session.

Three-minute demo
-----------------
1. Show Aisha at 3%, with her route turning red.
2. Evaluate three OCPI charger offers.
3. Block the closest offer after its final terms mutate.
4. Select the charger maximizing on-time arrival within S$12.
5. Pay on XRP Testnet; show ClawCredit only if the real path is proven.
6. Start the simulated OCPP charger and energy meter.
7. Turn the route green and display the combined delivery receipt.

Irreducibility test
-------------------
- Without the agent, this can collapse into a charger app. The demo must make
  the decision dynamic and multi-constraint while Aisha is driving: battery,
  queue, arrival time, connector, and final tariff must all matter.
- Cards already support ad hoc charging. The business model must be direct
  vehicle-to-operator commerce without a prior account or roaming contract,
  with a machine-verifiable exact quote and immediate settlement.

Primary risks
-------------
- Use an OCPP simulator or small hardware light/meter; do not imply a physical
  charger integration that does not exist.
- Card and QR payments are serious substitutes.
- Payment regulation, refunds, energy resale, and pricing rules vary by market.
- The AI role must include interpreting Aisha's changing schedule and
  negotiating heterogeneous offers, not merely sorting chargers by distance.

Strategic score: 41/45
Best quality: strongest stage theatre and most intuitive ClawCredit story.
