1. JULIUS BAER
==========================================================================

What it is
----------
Julius Baer is presented as a Swiss, publicly listed, pure-play wealth
management firm. In this context, it is the sponsor/owner of a wealth-advice
hackathon challenge. The organization is not presented as a general retail
bank: the slides stress an exclusive strategic focus on wealth management,
with advice, investing, financing, and long-term client relationships at its
core.

What it appears to value
-------------------------
- Trust built over time. The firm points to a Swiss banking history dating to
  1890, its family-business heritage, profitability, balance-sheet strength,
  and capitalisation. These are signals that durability, soundness, and client
  confidence matter.
- Personal service. A relationship manager (RM) should be located close to
  clients and backed by specialists. Advice should be tailored to clients’
  aspirations, and relationships should last across generations.
- Human judgement at the centre. The challenge explicitly asks teams to
  “preserve the RM’s central role.” AI is therefore expected to augment the
  adviser rather than replace the adviser or make opaque autonomous decisions.
- Specialist, holistic wealth advice. The offering spans wealth planning,
  investing, and financing, supported by proprietary research with a global
  perspective and comprehensive investment expertise.
- Global reach with local relevance. The slides describe operations in about
  25 countries and 60 locations, seven booking centres worldwide, and external
  expert partnerships. The intended model is local client proximity supported
  by international centres of expertise.
- Explainability and suitability. In the challenge, a useful answer must make
  the “why” behind a portfolio outcome understandable, relate it to the
  client’s actual holdings, and respect portfolio suitability.

Business/problem context
------------------------
Julius Baer’s stated challenge is “From Portfolio Monitoring to Intelligence.”
It wants an AI-powered wealth-intelligence layer that turns portfolio data into
advice. Instead of leaving an RM with a dashboard full of positions and market
movements, the product should help the RM answer three client-relevant
questions:

1. “What does my client’s portfolio look like?”
2. “What should I know?”
3. “What should I do next?”

The core opportunity is to translate financial data and external events into
well-justified, client-specific actions while keeping the professional adviser
accountable for the advice.

Requested solution building blocks
----------------------------------
1. Intelligent Portfolio Explanations
   Explain what a portfolio did and why, connecting real market and
   geopolitical events to the individual holdings that moved. A strong product
   would therefore avoid generic market commentary and show the causal chain
   from event -> holding exposure -> portfolio impact -> client-relevant
   implication.

2. Proactive Risk and Opportunity Detection
   Surface concentration, liquidity, currency, and mandate risks, alongside
   event-driven ideas, before the client has to ask. “Proactive” is important:
   the system should alert and prioritise, rather than merely respond to a
   query. It must still be sufficiently explainable for the RM to assess and
   communicate the result.

3. RM Intelligence Workbench
   Convert insight into client-ready actions, including rebalancing, tax-aware
   optimisation, stress testing, and life-event planning. This implies a single
   workflow for evidence, recommendation, review, and advisor/client action.

How work is judged
------------------
The Julius Baer slide lists four judging criteria:

- Client-centric innovation: solve a meaningful problem for the end client,
  not merely demonstrate a technical capability.
- User experience and design: make the product legible and useful to the RM
  and, where relevant, the client.
- Technical and operational feasibility: show a credible build and operating
  path, not only a conceptual AI experience.
- Strategic impact: demonstrate why the idea would materially improve Julius
  Baer’s wealth-management proposition.

The presentation of the prototype itself affects the score. A polished,
end-to-end demonstration matters.

Practical product implications (interpretation)
------------------------------------------------
- Keep an RM approval/review step before actions or recommendations are sent to
  a client.
- Make every recommendation traceable to portfolio facts, data sources,
  assumptions, client mandate, and the market/event rationale.
- Use a client profile, risk tolerance, liquidity needs, tax context, currency
  exposure, and life goals as constraints—not merely portfolio performance.
- Prioritise exceptions and materially relevant insights, since RMs need help
  focusing attention rather than more alerts.
- Frame AI as a research and decision-support copilot, consistent with the
  relationship-led service model.

2. RIPPLE / XRP LEDGER (XRPL)
==========================================================================

What it is
----------
The slides present the XRP Ledger (XRPL) as a programmable ledger platform
with a single API surface through which an application can reach multiple
ledger functions. It is the primary technical build path for the agentic
payments challenge. “Ripple” appears in the GitHub path shown on the slide
(SingHacks-2026/ripple), while the product slide specifically names the XRP
Ledger.

What the slides emphasise
-------------------------
- Developer accessibility: “A few lines of JavaScript, Python or Java reach
  every feature of the ledger.” The diagram also lists SDK support for JS,
  Python, Java, Rust, and more.
- A broad function set behind one endpoint/API: payments, NFTs, escrow and
  checks, decentralised identity (DID), credentials, fungible tokens, and a
  DEX/AMM-related feature (partly obscured in the photograph).
- Composability: the proposition is that an app can combine payment,
  programmable-value, identity, credential, and settlement-oriented ledger
  capabilities rather than integrate a different service for every function.

Why this matters in the hackathon
---------------------------------
XRPL is positioned as the execution rail for a complete commercial loop:

customer need -> payment -> value delivered

The slide calls for a working AI-agent-powered prototype using the XRP Ledger,
XRPL AI Starter Kit, x402, MPP, and other specified resources. This means a
good prototype should show more than agent reasoning or a payment screen. It
should demonstrate an agent identifying/handling a real customer need,
executing an appropriately controlled payment, and delivering a tangible
resulting service or product.

Values and design signals (interpretation)
------------------------------------------
- Programmability and low-friction integration.
- Payment finality/execution linked to applications rather than isolated
  blockchain activity.
- Identity and credentials as potential trust controls for agentic commerce.
- A broad, developer-facing toolkit designed for real application flows.

3. THE FLOOR
==========================================================================

What it is
----------
The Floor is shown in a slide/video titled “Agentic AI in Insurance.” The
visible message is “One decision, followed all the way through.” The material
therefore presents The Floor as an insurance-focused agentic-AI/trust approach
or product example. The photos do not provide a formal company description,
founding details, or product catalogue, so this brief does not assume more
than the evidence supports.

What it appears to value
-------------------------
The slide makes five explicit trust properties prominent:

- Attributable: a decision can be connected to a responsible actor, source, or
  identifiable provenance.
- Detectable: relevant changes, deviations, or problems can be observed.
- Bounded: the system’s authority/scope has defined limits rather than open-
  ended autonomy.
- No single trust root: trust does not rely solely on one opaque authority or
  central point of failure.
- Demonstrable: the organisation can show evidence of how a decision and its
  downstream steps occurred.

The image pairs a “Decision” identifier with a sequence of records and a
“Drift” line. The intended idea appears to be that a decision is traceable
through its lifecycle and that post-decision changes or model/process drift can
be detected.

Why it is relevant to agentic payments (interpretation)
--------------------------------------------------------
The Floor’s framing supplies a useful trust model for any autonomous agent
that can cause financial or insurance outcomes. An agent should not simply be
able to act; its authority should be limited, its actions recorded, the path
from decision to outcome inspectable, and unexpected drift detectable. For a
hackathon prototype, this could translate into a decision receipt/audit trail,
explicit spending and action bounds, an approval policy, and evidence that the
fulfilled service matches the agent’s authorised intent.

==========================================================================

The images also cite several standards, initiatives, or companies. They appear
as context for the market’s current “lots of specs, SDKs, and hype” stage. They
are not necessarily sponsors or instructions to use every one of them.

OpenAI — Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP)
-----------------------------------------
The slide cites “OpenAI Developers” and “Agentic Commerce Protocol,” labelled
ACP. In the presentation’s argument, it is an example of an emerging protocol
or interface for agent-enabled commerce. The broader takeaway is that agentic
commerce is attracting standards and developer tooling, but a convincing
product still needs a concrete real-world use case and trust model.

Google Cloud — Agent Payments Protocol (AP2)
---------------------------------------------
The slide cites a Google Cloud announcement for “Agent Payments Protocol
(AP2).” It is used as another signal that agent-payment standards are emerging.
The exact visible wording of the headline is “Announcing Agent Payments
Protocol (AP2).” It is part of the standards landscape, not the XRPL-specific
build requirement shown in the challenge photo.

Mastercard — Verifiable Intent
------------------------------
The photo includes a Mastercard item stating, in substance, that when AI buys
for a person, trust becomes the product, and introduces “Verifiable Intent,” a
standards-based trust paradigm for agentic commerce co-developed with Google.
This is conceptually important: an autonomous payment should carry evidence of
what the principal intended, so merchants/payment participants can distinguish
legitimate delegated action from unauthorised activity.

OpenWallet Standard
-------------------
Listed as an example of a payment/agent ecosystem standard. The slide gives no
further details. Its inclusion reinforces the theme that wallet and payment
interoperability are active areas of standardisation.

Virtuals Protocol
-----------------
Listed as an ecosystem example. The photos do not give its role or required
integration; treat it as market context only.

x402 and MPP
------------
Shown as prominent protocol labels. The XRPL challenge slide specifically asks
teams to use x402 and MPP (as well as the XRPL AI Starter Kit), so these are
part of the requested build path. The “status quo” slide places them in the

Agent2Agent (A2A) and Universal Commerce Protocol

Shown as further ecosystem terms/standards. The photo does not contain

5. SYNTHESIS: WHAT A STRONG HACKATHON CONCEPT WOULD SHOW
==========================================================================

Across the photos, the common message is not “put an AI agent on a payment
rail.” It is to create a bounded, trusted, end-to-end business experience:

1. Start with a real customer problem.
2. Let an AI-native agent understand the need and propose/choose an action
   within clear authority and policy limits.
3. Make intent, identity, evidence, and authorisation visible.
4. Execute the payment through the required XRPL-oriented technical path.
5. Deliver a concrete service or outcome after payment.
6. Retain a demonstrable audit trail from the original decision through every
   material action and outcome, including drift/exception monitoring.

For Julius Baer specifically, the equivalent pattern is: client portfolio and
goals -> explainable insight -> RM review -> tailored recommendation/action ->
transparent, client-ready rationale. The strongest unifying principle across
Julius Baer, XRPL, and The Floor is trustworthy autonomy: technology should
make decisions and payments more useful and faster, while preserving human
accountability, suitability, evidence, and control.
