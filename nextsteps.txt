ResearchAgent — next steps after hackathon baseline

Baseline
--------
The winning hackathon prototype is frozen at the git tag:

    hackathon-baseline

Keep fixture mode working throughout the next three months. New work should
be demonstrably better than this baseline, not merely different.

2. Define the three-month target
---------------------------------
Choose the primary product direction before expanding the system:

- Research-quality product
- Evidence marketplace and payment demonstration
- General-purpose research assistant

Recommended product promise:

    Research that can justify why it spent money.

Define:

- The primary user and their most important recurring research decision.
- The smallest valuable end-to-end workflow for that user.
- Three measurable outcomes for the three-month period.
- Explicit non-goals so the product does not become a generic chat surface.

3. Build an evaluation harness
------------------------------
Add repeatable evaluation before adding live sources or more agent behavior.
Measure:

- Retrieval relevance and ranking stability.
- Evidence-family and duplicate detection.
- Purchase decisions under different budgets.
- Citation validity and accessible evidence spans.
- Whether paid evidence materially changes the conclusion.
- Fixture determinism and fallback behavior.

Keep a small golden dataset of questions, expected evidence families, blocked
offers, and acceptable conclusions. Treat quality regressions as release
blockers.

4. Make the data model trustworthy
-----------------------------------
There are currently two source definitions: the 20-record JSON corpus and a
12-record hardcoded fallback in the TypeScript domain file.

- Select one canonical source-of-truth format.
- Keep premium bodies server-only.
- Validate source records at startup and in tests.
- Version source metadata, evidence spans, ranking logic, and fixture content.
- Preserve publisher, provenance, family, access, price, and license state.

5. Add real source adapters carefully
-------------------------------------
Start with public metadata and openly accessible material.

- Define a source-adapter interface with timeouts and graceful fallback.
- Begin with stable public APIs or feeds rather than scraping.
- Store provenance, retrieval time, source URL, license, and content hash.
- Cache normalized evidence so runs are reproducible.
- Keep the website allowlist and source policy server-enforced.
- Do not bypass paywalls or redistribute copyrighted article bodies.

6. Strengthen persistence and payments
--------------------------------------
Do this after the evaluation and data foundations are stable.

- Replace JSON run storage with durable transactional persistence.
- Add authentication, authorization, retention, and rate limits.
- Model quotes, invoices, entitlements, access grants, and settlement state
  as separate records.
- Enforce quote expiry, resource binding, replay protection, and idempotency.
- Add structured logs and operational error visibility.
- Keep XRPL spending restricted to explicitly authorized Testnet flows until
  the entitlement and payment model is proven.

7. Validate with real researchers
---------------------------------
Run discovery and usability sessions with approximately 5–10 target users.

- Observe how they formulate questions and choose sources.
- Test whether the budget changes their trust or workflow in a useful way.
- Compare open-only and paid-evidence dossiers.
- Record where users distrust the ranking, purchase rationale, or citations.
- Turn repeated findings into product decisions and evaluation cases.

Supporting priorities
---------------------

- Preserve the current fixture demo as a fast, no-credential smoke test.
- Keep the distinction between evidence, payment, access, and synthesis clear.
- Prefer a narrow, reliable research workflow over additional agent personas.
- Avoid LangChain, hosted vector databases, generic crawling, and mainnet
  payments until there is a demonstrated need.
- Avoid UI polish that is not tied to a measured workflow problem.
- Keep product, security, provenance, and payment decisions documented briefly
  as they become stable.

Suggested sequence
------------------

Weeks 1–2: product target, success metrics, golden evaluation set.
Weeks 3–4: canonical data model and deterministic quality improvements.
Weeks 5–8: first public source adapters and provenance/caching.
Weeks 9–10: persistence, access control, and payment hardening.
Weeks 11–12: researcher validation, iteration, and release decision.

Open decisions
--------------

- Which user and recurring research decision are primary?
- Which three metrics define success?
- What evidence sources are legally and operationally safe to support?
- Is XRPL a core user value or an enabling payment rail?
- What must be true before leaving fixture mode?


Hackathon presentation plan: two-month scope
=============================================

This section supersedes the three-month sequence above for the next eight
weeks. It is optimized for four people contributing approximately one combined
team-hour per day. That is roughly 40–56 real team-hours over two months,
depending on whether work happens on weekdays or every day. LLM assistance may
accelerate implementation, but it does not remove integration, debugging,
deployment, or rehearsal time.

Product decision
----------------

Do not build a general-purpose Perplexity competitor. The demo product is:

    An agent that proves why a specific piece of paid evidence was worth
    buying, and shows exactly how that evidence changed the conclusion.

The primary user is an investment-research analyst preparing a decision brief.
The recurring decision is whether the evidence is strong enough to support an
investment-committee discussion. The canonical question remains:

    Is the AI data-centre investment boom sustainable through 2028?

The product promise for the presentation is:

    Research that can justify why it spent money.

Demo story
----------

1. The analyst sets a question, decision context, source allowlist, total
   budget, per-source ceiling, and approval rule.
2. The agent retrieves open evidence and groups duplicate reporting into
   evidence families.
3. The agent explains the unresolved evidence gap and ranks premium candidates
   by expected marginal value, not by price or popularity alone.
4. The user approves one affordable source. A redundant source is skipped and
   an over-ceiling source is blocked.
5. The purchase/entitlement boundary is shown, using fixture settlement by
   default and optional XRPL Testnet settlement in a controlled environment.
6. The purchased evidence changes the dossier from a broad demand thesis to a
   more cautious conclusion about grid connection and operating capacity.
7. The user opens an evidence receipt showing claims, exact spans, provenance,
   spending, and settlement status.

Build in the two-month window
-----------------------------

- Keep one vertical and one canonical scenario. Add no general web-search
  surface until the demo path is reliable.
- Make `data/mock-articles.json` the single fixture source of truth. Remove or
  isolate the hard-coded fallback records in `src/domain.ts` so tests cannot
  silently exercise a different corpus.
- Add a visible spending mandate: total budget, per-source cap, allowed source
  profiles, expiry, and human approval state.
- Make the before/after conclusion the central UI moment. The viewer should be
  able to see what was known before the purchase, what the paid source added,
  and what conclusion changed.
- Add an Evidence Receipt export (JSON plus print-friendly HTML/PDF). Include
  source URL, publisher, retrieval time, content hash, access/licence state,
  family ID, exact evidence-span IDs, claim links, quote/invoice ID, and
  fixture or Testnet settlement status. Do not export premium article bodies.
- Add deterministic failure paths: LLM unavailable, Testnet unavailable,
  budget exhausted, duplicate source, source above the mandate ceiling, and
  invalid/stale purchase.
- Provide two modes from one codebase:
  - local: one-command fixture demo with no credentials;
  - online: the same build/container, fixture mode by default, with a visible
    demo/simulated-settlement banner.
- Add a reset/demo-seed action so the presentation can always return to the
  canonical state.

Payment and agent standards
----------------------------

The current implementation should be described as x402-style until it speaks
the actual wire protocol. If the judges require an x402 demonstration, add one
protected evidence endpoint that returns a real HTTP 402 challenge, binds the
quote to the resource, verifies the retry, and returns the evidence only after
the payment/fixture check. Do not build a general facilitator or multi-chain
payment layer.

Use a small evidence model inspired by the W3C Web Annotation Data Model:

- TextQuoteSelector: exact excerpt plus prefix and suffix;
- TextPositionSelector: offsets where available;
- canonical URL, retrieval timestamp, content hash, and access state;
- source family and claim-to-span links.

Use lightweight provenance fields inspired by W3C PROV-O, such as
`wasDerivedFrom`, `wasQuotedFrom`, `generatedAtTime`, and `wasAssociatedWith`.
These are useful export conventions; do not introduce an RDF infrastructure
project.

AP2-style intent/mandate language may be used to explain the user-approved
budget and resource binding, but AP2 itself should not be implemented. MCP,
A2A, OAuth, multi-agent delegation, and tool registries are post-hackathon
work.

Publisher simulation and content policy
----------------------------------------

Do not use a 12ft-style prefix or another paywall bypass. Do not scrape,
redistribute, or imply a commercial Financial Times partnership.

Use a clearly labelled `Partner Demo Sandbox` with fictional publisher names
and synthetic or deliberately supplied sample text. Show the same metadata,
quote, payment, entitlement, and controlled-excerpt flow that a licensed
publisher adapter would use later. The interface should state that it is a
simulation and that no real publisher payment or FT article body is involved.

Explicit non-goals
------------------

- general-purpose crawling or browsing;
- embeddings, a hosted vector database, LangChain, or agent personas;
- multi-user accounts, subscriptions, production billing, or mainnet funds;
- real publisher onboarding or content licensing;
- AP2/MCP/A2A compliance claims;
- a second research vertical before the first one is presentation-ready.

Eight-week sequence
-------------------

Week 1: freeze the pitch, canonical question, mandate, success criteria, and
        exact demo script.
Week 2: canonicalize fixture data and add tests for source/family/ranking
        determinism.
Week 3: implement claim-to-span evidence receipts and before/after evidence
        impact in the dossier UI.
Week 4: add the local one-command mode, production build, Docker packaging,
        health check, and online deployment using the same image.
Week 5: add the mandate/approval interaction and one honest x402-style or
        x402-conformant protected-resource path, depending on judge needs.
Week 6: add failure states, reset/demo seed, accessibility checks, and clear
        fixture/Testnet/LLM status labels.
Week 7: run the complete story repeatedly on both local and hosted versions;
        fix only observed friction and reliability failures.
Week 8: reserve for rehearsal, screenshots/video, deployment recovery, and
        final verification. Do not start a new major feature.

Definition of done
------------------

- A judge can open the hosted URL without credentials and complete the story.
- The same story works locally with no network or API dependency in fixture
  mode.
- Every displayed claim links to an exact evidence span.
- The app visibly distinguishes open evidence, paid evidence, payment, access,
  and synthesis.
- The purchase decision is bounded by a server-side mandate and is idempotent.
- A reset restores the canonical run in under one minute.
- The team can honestly explain which parts are simulated and which, if any,
  are XRPL Testnet-validated.

Post-hackathon backlog
----------------------

Only after the demo is stable: one authorised public-source adapter, durable
transactional persistence, authentication/retention/rate limits, licensed
publisher integrations, conformance testing against a real x402 facilitator,
and researcher usability sessions. These are productisation steps, not required
for the presentation.
